## Purpose of the Event

FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week was the final showcase of the Build Week program, where participating teams presented Agentic AI solutions developed during a 24-hour Hackathon on the Amazon Web Services (AWS) platform.

The event was organized to provide students and technology enthusiasts with the opportunity to observe the complete development process of an AI-powered product, from identifying real-world problems and designing system architectures to building a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) and presenting the final solution to a panel of experts.

In addition to showcasing technical solutions, the event also shared valuable practical experiences in teamwork, time management, and product-oriented thinking under high-pressure development environments.

---

## Participating Teams

The teams presenting their projects at the event included:

- Team One
- Team 2K
- Team Six Piller

Each team introduced an Agentic AI solution designed to solve real business challenges by integrating multiple AWS services.

---

## Key Topics

### Practical Hackathon Experience

The event began with participating teams sharing the lessons they learned after developing their projects within only 24 hours.

Some key takeaways included:

- Always begin with the customer's real pain point instead of focusing only on technology.
- A stable and functional Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is far more valuable than an impressive idea that exists only in presentation slides.
- Team discussions are necessary when selecting the best solution, but quick decision-making and execution are essential to meet tight deadlines.
- Effective time management plays a critical role in the success of a Hackathon project.

The teams emphasized that understanding user requirements correctly is the foundation for building valuable and practical solutions.

---

### Team One – Automated Enterprise Data Collection System

Team One presented an Agentic AI system designed to help non-technical users collect and analyze enterprise data.

The system architecture was built using:

- React
- AWS Amplify
- Amazon Cognito
- AWS Lambda

The solution employed an AI Supervisor responsible for coordinating multiple specialized Sub-Agents.

These Sub-Agents automatically collected and analyzed financial reports and company information before generating summarized insights for managers.

The presentation demonstrated that Agentic AI is capable of coordinating multiple intelligent agents to complete complex business workflows rather than performing only individual tasks.

---

### Team 2K – AWS Architecture Assistant

Team 2K introduced an AI-powered assistant designed for Solution Architects and Cloud Engineers.

The project addressed the challenge that customers often require cloud architecture designs and cost estimations within a very limited timeframe.

Their solution allows users to describe requirements using natural language.

The system automatically:

- Analyzes user requirements.
- Generates AWS architecture diagrams.
- Estimates deployment costs.
- Produces Infrastructure as Code (IaC).
- Supports direct deployment to AWS.

This solution significantly reduces work that previously required many hours or even several days of manual effort.

---

### Team Six Piller – Adaptive Workflow Engine

Team Six Piller introduced an Agentic AI solution for the financial industry to support Anti-Money Laundering (AML) investigations.

The system consists of multiple AI Agents responsible for different tasks, including:

- Know Your Customer (KYC) verification.
- Transaction analysis.
- Evidence collection.
- Investigation support and decision-making.

To ensure security and auditability, the solution integrates:

- AWS Security Hub
- AWS Key Management Service (KMS)

In addition to generating investigation results, the system stores complete reasoning logs to explain every AI decision, helping organizations satisfy auditing and regulatory requirements in the financial sector.

---

### Challenges During the Hackathon

All participating teams agreed that the greatest challenge was not the technology itself, but developing a complete product within only 24 hours.

Some of the major challenges included:

- Time pressure.
- Managing cloud infrastructure.
- Dividing tasks among team members.
- Resolving unexpected technical issues during development.
- Ensuring system stability during the live demonstration.

These experiences provided participants with a better understanding of real-world software development under tight deadlines.

## Knowledge Gained

### Product-Oriented Thinking

Through the project presentations, I realized that identifying the right problem to solve is always more important than selecting the latest technology.

A successful product should deliver real value to users rather than simply incorporating many modern technologies.

---

### Agentic AI

The presentations helped me better understand the architecture of Agentic AI.

Instead of relying on a single AI model, an Agentic AI system can consist of multiple specialized agents working together to solve complex tasks.

This represents one of the most promising directions for modern AI application development.

---

### Real-World AWS Architecture

The event provided an opportunity to observe how different teams combined AWS services such as:

- AWS Amplify
- Amazon Cognito
- AWS Lambda
- Amazon Bedrock
- AWS Security Hub
- AWS Key Management Service (KMS)

to build complete cloud-native systems that address practical business challenges.

---

### Teamwork

One of the most valuable lessons was the importance of collaboration among team members.

Proper task allocation, continuous communication, and mutual support enabled teams to complete their projects successfully despite the extremely limited development time.

---

## Applying the Knowledge

The knowledge gained from this event can be directly applied to my Polly Voice project.

### MVP-Oriented Development

The participating teams emphasized that delivering a stable and functional product is more important than attempting to implement too many features.

This encourages me to focus on completing the core functionalities of Polly Voice before expanding the application with additional features.

---

### Serverless Architecture Design

The fact that several teams selected AWS Amplify, Amazon Cognito, and AWS Lambda further confirmed that a Serverless architecture is an excellent choice for modern web applications.

This also reinforced my decision to build Polly Voice using AWS Serverless services.

---

### Problem-Solving Mindset

The presentations taught me that application development should always begin with real user requirements.

For Polly Voice, the goal is not only to convert text into speech but also to provide a simple, user-friendly experience that effectively meets users' needs.

---

### Working Under Pressure

The Hackathon demonstrated that software development is always accompanied by time constraints and unexpected technical issues.

This experience highlighted the importance of project planning, time management, and maintaining a calm mindset when facing challenges during software development.

---

## Event Experience

FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week was one of the most inspiring events I attended throughout my internship journey.

What impressed me the most was not only the innovative AI solutions presented by the teams but also their honest discussions about the challenges, failures, and lessons they experienced during the Hackathon.

Watching the teams explain their system architectures, AWS service selections, and approaches to solving real-world business problems gave me a much clearer understanding of the complete AI product development lifecycle.

Most importantly, I realized that the success of a project depends not only on technology but also on teamwork, effective time management, and a user-centered mindset.

The event also inspired me with many new ideas for integrating AWS services to build more advanced AI applications in the future.
